Activity
Paper trading: no real orders are sent. Every new position passes the risk agent (lot limits, margin, entry window) and gets an automatic premium stop-loss. Research tool, not investment advice.

What the words mean

Call (CE)
Gains when the index rises. Buying a call = bullish. Selling a call = you expect it NOT to rise above the strike.
Put (PE)
Gains when the index falls. Buying a put = bearish. Selling a put = you expect it NOT to fall below the strike.
Strike
The index level the option is based on.
Price / premium (LTP)
Last traded price of one unit. Total cost = price x quantity (lots x lot size).
ATM / in-the-money
ATM = strike closest to the current price. In-the-money options already have value from the current price.
Open contracts (OI)
How many contracts are open at that strike. Big call OI often acts as resistance, big put OI as support.
PCR
Put OI divided by call OI. Above 1: more puts written (traders expect support). Below 1: more calls written.
Max pain
The expiry level where option buyers in total would gain the least. Prices sometimes drift toward it near expiry.
Expected move (straddle)
ATM call + put price. Roughly how far the market expects the index could move by expiry.
IV
Implied volatility: how much movement the option prices assume. Higher IV = costlier options.
Breakeven
Index level at expiry where the trade makes zero before charges.
MTM
Mark-to-market: today's booked plus open profit/loss, after charges.
Margin
Money blocked by the broker. Buying options blocks the premium; selling blocks much more (about 12% of index value per unit).
Stop-loss (SL)
Price at which a position closes automatically to limit the loss.
